title: Economic Loss Due to Health Funding Cuts as Distributed Across Geospatial Units

order: 4
---
In a [preprint manuscript](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.07.24.25332092v2), SCIMaP Senior Data Analyst Dr. Harris and colleagues assessed the economic impact of NIH policy changes that slashed research infrastructure rates and HHS terminations and freezes of more than 5,000 grants between February and August 2025. Nationally, the researchers estimate $16.6 billion in future losses from the proposed indirect cost cap and $11 billion in losses from terminated and frozen grants as of August 2025. These impacts extend well beyond research institutions. Reducing science funding has knock-on effects for local communities—cutting salaries, purchasing power, and tax revenue. Nearly 70% of counties nationwide would experience over $100,000 in economic losses. The commuter flow analysis illustrates how cuts radiate outward: 2,136 counties would lose more than $100,000 from the indirect cost cap alone, even though only 376 counties contain NIH-funded institutions. Similarly, 989 counties are affected by grant terminations or freezes, compared to just 152 counties where affected institutions are located. As this preprint shows, cuts to biomedical research funding ripple far from the lab bench, reducing economic activity and costing jobs.
