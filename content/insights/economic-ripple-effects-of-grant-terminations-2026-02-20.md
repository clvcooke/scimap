When a federal research grant is terminated, the immediate impact is obvious: a lab loses funding, experiments stop, and researchers face uncertainty. But the economic consequences ripple far beyond the walls of any single institution.

## Beyond the Lab

Every dollar of NIH funding generates additional economic activity in the surrounding community. Research institutions purchase supplies from local vendors, employ staff who spend their salaries locally, and attract talent that contributes to the broader economy.

## Measuring the Ripple

Our analysis tracks the downstream effects of grant terminations across several dimensions:

- **Direct job losses** at research institutions
- **Supply chain impacts** on vendors and service providers
- **Reduced consumer spending** as displaced workers cut back
- **Long-term talent drain** as researchers relocate or leave the field

## Case Studies

Across the country, communities are feeling the effects of recent grant terminations. From university towns in the Midwest to research hubs on the coasts, the pattern is consistent: when federal science funding disappears, local economies suffer.

## The Path Forward

Understanding these ripple effects is the first step toward building the case for sustained science investment. By quantifying the full economic impact of grant terminations, we can have more informed conversations about the true cost of cuts to federal research funding.
