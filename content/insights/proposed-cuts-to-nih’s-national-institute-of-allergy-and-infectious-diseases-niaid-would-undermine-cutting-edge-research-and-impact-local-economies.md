The National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases (NIAID) at the National Institutes of Health (NIH) supports research that has led to high profile [medical success stories](https://web.archive.org/web/20250826162532/https://www.niaid.nih.gov/research/accomplishments). NIAID-supported research has contributed to life-saving treatments for HIV/AIDS, mRNA vaccines for the COVID-19 pandemic, and the development of drugs to delay the onset of Type 1 diabetes. But NIAID’s track record of supporting medical breakthroughs is threatened by budget cuts proposed in the White House’s [Fiscal Year (FY) 2027 budget request](https://www.niaid.nih.gov/sites/default/files/niaid-fy27-cj.pdf).

The White House’s proposed cut of $1.8 billion to NIAID in FY 2027 from FY 2025 levels could affect research into diseases and conditions that span tuberculosis, asthma, HIV/AIDS, pandemics, autoimmune diseases, and allergies. An analysis by the Science and Community Impacts Mapping Project (SCIMaP) shows that NIAID budget reductions would have negative economic impacts in communities across the U.S. through a combined loss of jobs and other research-associated economic activity, with total estimated economic losses of $6.4 billion compared to inflation-adjusted baseline NIAID support from 2020-2024.

**How NIAID cuts would impact research areas**

NIAID supports basic and applied research across infectious, autoimmune, and allergic diseases. NIAID currently funds more than [8,000 active](http://reporter.nih.gov) research projects. Major research activities include supporting a [center](https://reporter.nih.gov/search/JbRgAtchAUKHOoW2X1vObQ/project-details/10514317) for antiviral medicines and pandemic preparedness, developing clinical trials for next-generation [coronavirus vaccines](https://reporter.nih.gov/search/JbRgAtchAUKHOoW2X1vObQ/project-details/10887806), and identifying safe, effective, and scalable [interventions for HIV prevention](https://reporter.nih.gov/search/JbRgAtchAUKHOoW2X1vObQ/project-details/11237571) in U.S. communities and abroad.

NIH data shows average spending from FY 2018 to FY 2024 exceeded $1 billion (adjusted for inflation to 2025 dollars) in ten NIAID-associated research categories: infectious diseases, emerging infectious diseases, clinical research, prevention, biotechnology, biodefense, immunization, HIV/AIDS, vaccines, and rare diseases. 

The proposed FY 2027 budget cut would impact the broad scope of the NIAID research portfolio. The White House budget proposal would reduce the capacity of NIAID to fund competitively awarded extramural research projects conducted at universities, hospitals, and research institutes nationwide. The proposal decreases NIAID’s extramural budget for HIV/AIDS research from 2025’s $1.56 billion to $1.06 billion; biodefense and emerging infectious diseases research from $1.95 billion to $1.32 billion; and general infectious and immunological disease research from $1.77 billion to $1.2 billion. 

**Proposed NIAID cuts would cause economic losses nationwide**

Leveraging a geospatial analytics framework, SCIMaP has projected the economic impacts of the White House’s proposed NIAID budget cuts for FY 2027 at the local and regional levels. Estimated losses would exceed $25 million in 64 congressional districts and exceed $1 million in 417 of the 436 total districts. SCIMaP estimates that these cuts would lead to more than 100 lost jobs in each of 52 districts. Total job losses could exceed 22,000 nationwide compared to the 2020-2024 baseline average.

Economic losses will impact red, blue, and purple districts across the country. SCIMaP analysis, which assumes that economic impacts are dispersed regionally based on where individuals live and work, shows losses induced by NIAID budget cuts concentrated in multiple regions, particularly communities surrounding academic and medical research hubs like Seattle (including the University of Washington and the Fred Hutchinson Cancer Center), Houston (including Baylor University and the University of Texas Health Science Center), Atlanta (including Emory University, Georgia Tech, and Georgia State), the Durham-Chapel Hill metropolitan area (including Duke University and the University of North Carolina, Chapel Hill), and Boston (including Harvard and its affiliated medical centers in addition to MIT, Northeastern, and other major institutions). These five regions would each stand to lose over $200 million in economic activity, with Seattle topping the list at almost $600 million. In total, over $1.7 billion of economic loss is expected from these regions alone.
