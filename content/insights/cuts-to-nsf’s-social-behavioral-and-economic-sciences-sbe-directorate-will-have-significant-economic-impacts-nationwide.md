The U.S. National Science Foundation ([NSF](https://www.nsf.gov/)) is a major funding source for all areas of science and engineering including the social, behavioural, and economic sciences. The NSF’s Directorate for Social, Behavioral and Economic Sciences [(SBE)](https://www.nsf.gov/sbe) supports a broad range of disciplines including psychology, cognitive neuroscience, geography, anthropology, linguistics, economics, sociology, political science, and more.

According to the [Consortium of Social Science Associations](https://cossa.org/social-science/), most research funding for the social sciences in academic institutions comes from the NSF and originates from the SBE directorate within the NSF. But the White House’s 2027 budget proposal would **eliminate funding for almost all SBE-affiliated research**, leaving many social science fields without any alternative sources of federal funding.

NSF-supported [SBE researchers](https://web.archive.org/web/20260602014758/https://www.nsf.gov/sbe) have used economic methods to optimize the kidney donation process, analyzed neural activity in the brain to learn about cognition, and explored the origins of human society. SBE funding has enabled significant research breakthroughs, informed a broad range of policy goals, and funded the work of multiple [recent Nobel Prize winners.](https://www.nsf.gov/news/nsf-congratulates-laureates-2024-nobel-prize-economics) 

**What would happen if SBE funding is eliminated?**

Economic analysis by the Science and Community Impacts Mapping Project (SCIMaP) shows that eliminating SBE would result in substantial economic losses for U.S. communities nationwide. The results are adjusted for where researchers work and live and account for more than just the raw funding lost: localities would also lose jobs and the economic boost catalyzed by federal investment in research and development.

In 221 congressional districts, SCIMaP estimates the economic loss due to the elimination of SBE would exceed $1 million annually. In total, SCIMaP estimates that 4,000 jobs would be eliminated and $756 million of economic activity would be lost annually in communities nationwide. The elimination of SBE represents a significant departure from historical norms.

Between 2017 and 2025, Congress appropriated around $250-300 million each year to SBE, with budget requests falling in a similar range. In [FY 2026](https://www.nsf.gov/about/budget/fy2026), the White House proposed to slash SBE funds to $96 million, and then proposed to go further, reducing SBE-associated funds in [FY 2027](https://www.nsf.gov/about/budget/fy2027) to $44 million. In doing so, the White House has proposed to dissolve the SBE Directorate and direct the remaining funds to a newly separate entity: the ([National Center for Science and Engineering Statistics](https://ncses.nsf.gov/)), responsible for collecting and analyzing data about the broad state of the science and engineering enterprise. All other SBE research funding would be eliminated, leaving many fields without alternative sources of federal funding.

**Case Study: How losses could affect two Michigan districts** 

SCIMaP used baseline support (inflation adjusted and averaged over years 2020-2024) from the NSF’s SBE Directorate to assess the economic impact of the White House’s FY 2027 budget proposal. SCIMaP researchers found that economic losses associated with SBE elimination would be widespread and include districts that span partisan differences. This is especially true in Michigan’s 6th and 7th congressional districts, represented by Debbie Dingell (MI-06) and Tom Barrett (MI-07). The two districts have the greatest expected losses for a Democrat and Republican district, respectively: $33.8 million and 175 jobs for MI-06, and $10.7 million and 55 jobs for MI-07. 

Both districts are economically linked with public universities that receive SBE funding. For MI-06, that is driven by activities at the University of Michigan, Ann Arbor (UMich), and in MI-07, that is driven by activities at the Michigan State University and UMich. Other expected losses are associated with research at Eastern Michigan University and Wayne State University. 

The NSF currently funds a wide range of research projects at these institutions. UMich ongoing NSF-funded research activities include studying how languages evolve and how the gut microbiome responds to environmental change. At Michigan State, ongoing NSF-funded research activities include a project on the effects of COVID-19 on at-risk youth and using AI to understand the development of spatial thinking. 

The largest funded UMich SBE grant in the past six years is for over $38 million, supporting the implementation of a [national platform](https://www.nsf.gov/awardsearch/show-award?AWD_ID=1946932) for social and behavioral science data. The largest funded Michigan State SBE grant in the same time frame is for over $1.5 million for a project that examines how humans can [confront the impacts](https://www.nsf.gov/awardsearch/show-award?AWD_ID=2307313) that Earth’s changing environmental systems have on society. 

**Explore more on your own**

The national-scale analysis and two examples from Michigan illustrate the impact of dissolving SBE on local economic investment in communities in major cities, regional hubs, and near college towns nationwide.
