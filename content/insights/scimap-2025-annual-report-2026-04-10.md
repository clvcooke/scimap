The Science and Community Impacts Mapping Project (SCIMaP) team has published its 2025 Annual Report. 

SCIMaP launched in March 2025 after unprecedented cuts to federal funding for science began unfolding. Grant terminations, indirect cost cuts, and proposed budget reductions hit in quick succession, but there was no way to track what any of these cuts meant for communities across the country. The SCIMaP team took a different approach. The project translated public NIH and NSF funding data into real-time, community-level maps that showed the local economic impact of the cuts and what was at stake.

“Federal policy changes move fast, but their impacts on communities often take time to surface,” said ***Joshua Weitz, PhD, Co-Principal Investigator*** for SCIMaP. “That gap is exactly why we built SCIMaP. We wanted to make the local consequences of federal decisions visible and measurable.”

Over the past year, those maps reached millions of Americans, supported Congressional advocacy efforts, and informed the work of national science policy organizations. 

In addition, through interactive quizzes and maps, and multiple online surveys with national samples, the team was able to learn more about what influences people’s decisions to take action.

“We explored what Americans across the political spectrum think about science funding cuts, and how learning about local impact shapes attitudes and motivates action,” said ***Allie Sinclair, PhD, Co-Principal Investigator*** and lead of the behavioral science team. “In studies with more than 5,000 participants and 22,000 website visitors, we discovered that when people see what’s at stake in their own community, they disapprove of science funding cuts and intend to take action in support of science. Personalized, interactive tools like the SCIMaP impact maps did more to motivate action than simple messages, showing that active engagement is a step toward continued action.”

The annual report documents SCIMaP’s impact throughout 2025, spanning research, partnerships, engagement, and turns toward what comes next. Federal science funding is still at risk, with new challenges on the horizon.SCIMaP continues to address these challenges and communicate the consequences for communities and research institutions.
