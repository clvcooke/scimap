The Science & Community Impact Mapping Project (SCIMaP) has released its 2025 Annual Report, documenting a year of evidence-based analysis at the intersection of federal science policy and local economic impact.

## Background

SCIMaP launched in March 2025 in response to an unprecedented wave of grant terminations, indirect cost cuts, and proposed budget reductions. Our mission was clear: translate public data on NIH and NSF funding into real-time, community-level maps that could reach millions of Americans, inform Congressional advocacy, and support national science policy organizations.

## Key Findings

Over the past year, SCIMaP has:

- Mapped federal science funding impacts across all 50 states at the county, congressional district, and city level
- Reached millions of Americans through interactive data visualizations
- Provided data-driven insights to Congressional offices and advocacy organizations
- Published peer-reviewed research on the economic impacts of funding cuts

## What Comes Next

As federal science funding remains volatile and the stakes for local communities grow, SCIMaP will continue to expand its coverage, deepen its analysis, and build new tools for understanding the downstream effects of science policy decisions.

The full report details our research methodology, engagement metrics, and strategic roadmap for 2026.
