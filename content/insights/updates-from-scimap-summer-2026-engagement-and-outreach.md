For researchers at the Science and Community Impacts Mapping Project (SCIMaP), this past summer was an opportunity to get the word out about findings and make connections in academic and professional communities. From the team’s home base in Maryland, SCIMaP researchers presented findings at conferences, shared data resources, and engaged in dialogue with stakeholders to learn more about pressing needs and ways that SCIMaP can inform ongoing policy debates on the future of federal support of science. Here are some highlights from the last few months. 

**Presenting to Friends of NIAID** 

As part of SCIMaP’s research into the impacts of proposed cuts to the National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases (NIAID), SCIMaP researchers Clio Andris and Mallory Harris, lead GIS analyst and senior data analyst at SCIMaP, respectively, presented to Friends of NIAID, a coalition of advocates across the science and public health communities dedicated to supporting NIAID. The geographic-based datasets SCIMaP shared during this virtual meeting were the basis of our [August blog post](https://scienceimpacts.org/insights/NIAID-2026-08-17) about NIAID.

“The big takeaway that I had is how disproportionate cuts to NIAID have been relative to other institutes and centers of the National Institutes of Health (NIH),” says Harris. “NIAID is taking the largest absolute hit in the Fiscal Year 2027 proposed budget.” 

**Presenting at the International Conference on the Science of Science and Innovation**

This summer, Harris and SCIMaP data analyst Elena Parkerson attended the International Conference on the Science of Science in Boulder, Colorado. There, Harris presented an overview of the SCIMaP website and Parkerson gave a lightning talk on changes to multi-year funding, which SCIMaP is actively researching.

At the conference, Harris received a best speaker award, which Harris said was a “validation of the work we’ve been doing as a team and our contribution to science of science as a field.” Harris and Parkerson had the opportunity to network with other researchers investigating different disruptions at NIH, opening the potential for future collaboration and resource sharing. 

**Presenting at FreeState Justice's Pathway to Progress Event**

On July 24, Parkerson presented on a panel at FreeState Justice’s Pathway to Progress event in Baltimore, Maryland. FreeState Justice is a legal nonprofit in Maryland working to support the state’s LGBTQ+ community. Their 2026 Pathway to Progress event was a summit bringing together leaders in public health and the local community to confront barriers preventing LGBTQ+ Marylanders from accessing equitable healthcare. 

Parkerson explained how attendees advocating for HIV/AIDS support could use SCIMaP as a resource for their advocacy work, and also asked for input on the types of analyses that the audience was interested in. “People seemed mainly interested in hearing about disparate impacts across geographic regions and political leanings,” Parkerson says. A key takeaway from SCIMaP’s analysis of the geographic impact of grant terminations and policy changes is the extent to which federal funding cuts to science impact [blue, purple, and red districts alike](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.07.24.25332092v3).

**Meeting With Science Coalition Leaders**

SCIMaP leadership, including co-lead Allie Sinclair, were busy this summer meeting with leaders throughout the science advocacy space. Those organizations include the Federation of Associations in Behavioral & Brain Sciences (FABBS), the Coalition for National Science Funding (CNSF), and the Consortium of Social Science Associations (COSSA). For FABBS and COSSA leadership, Sinclair shared data and interactive maps featuring SCIMaP’s work on the [impacts of cuts](https://scienceimpacts.org/insights/SBE-2026-07-13) to the National Science Foundation’s Social, Behavioral and Economic Sciences (SBE) directorate. 

“As a researcher in psychology and neuroscience, the proposal to cut funding and dismantle SBE hit close to home,” Sinclair says. “We connected with organizations striving to save SBE to share SCIMaP resources and insights demonstrating the value of investing in the social sciences.” Similarly, Andris presented SCIMaP’s work at the June town hall meeting for COSSA members over Zoom, noting: “My experience was that COSSA is really leading advocacy for SBE. I haven't encountered another group that was dedicated right to this directorate, so they are at the epicenter.”

As part of a presentation to CNSF, the leadership team including co-lead Joshua Weitz, Sinclair, Harris, and Andris presented the rationale for SCIMaP, early findings on the impact of potential cuts to research infrastructure, and ongoing work to assess the economic impacts of grant terminations and the potential impacts of budget proposals. The team also shared active research showing what happens when the public engages with the economic consequences of federal cuts to science. As explored in a series of surveys and direct engagement work, the SCIMaP team has found that learning more about the local economic impacts of cuts tends to increase awareness, engagement, and inclination to take action—a message of broad relevance to policy makers and advocacy groups alike.
